How the material works

The stock is generated from overlapping curved fibers. Their density and direction guide a small moisture and heat field. The same field changes the surface relief, light response, burn damage, and sheet deformation. Pressed lettering compresses that grain. Water spreads, swells the sheet, and leaves marks as it dries. Heat chars dry fibers; moisture consumes heat first. These are simplified, deliberately visible experiments, not a calibrated paper or fire simulation.

This is a real, thin 3D solid. Its face, reverse, and edges turn together. Fine procedural grain perturbs the surface normals—the tiny directions that tell light how to bounce—so the paper responds to light instead of carrying a painted-on texture.

A separate softened height map gives the lettering a recessed edge: the ink sits inside the paper, and the surrounding rim casts a tiny directional shadow into each letter. Raking light makes those letter edges easier to see. A short ray march into the relief map also produces parallax: the recessed ink shifts relative to the paper rim as you tilt it. This is a depth shader, not individually modeled letter geometry. The modeled stock is approximately 0.3 mm thick at an assumed 89 mm card width; this is an illustrative paper thickness, not measured from the photo.
